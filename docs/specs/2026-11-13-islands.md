# Fur & Fang: the "Islands" update (spec, 2026-11-13)

Build-ready spec. No code here. It extends `docs/SPEC.md` (v1) and must fit the code as it stands at commit
`ad38b93`. Three features ship together, built in this order:

1. **Separation events**: water splits the valley into two or three populations that evolve on their own.
2. **"Can they still interbreed?"**: hidden differences build up while populations are apart; when the water
   drops, students see what happens to hybrid kits.
3. **Founder effect and drift**: a storm washes four rabbits onto a small islet; students predict, repeat with
   new seeds, and compare with the mainland.

Punctuated equilibrium and a deep-time fossil view are **not** in this update (see Future).

## 2. Learning goals

### 2.1 Syllabus fit

NSW Science 7-10 (2023), Stage 5, focus area **Genetics and evolutionary change** (outcome code SC5-GEV-01), plus
Working scientifically. The content points below are **paraphrased**; before any wording is shown to students the
owner should check it against the school program.

| Syllabus idea (paraphrased) | What the student does in Islands | Evidence they produce |
|---|---|---|
| Natural selection acts on variation that already exists, and can change a population over generations. | Flood the river, give each bank a different climate, watch the same starting alleles go different ways. | Side-by-side allele-frequency graphs; cause-of-death bars per bank. |
| Isolation plus time can lead to new species (speciation). | Keep two populations apart for 10, 30 and 60 generations and test-cross them each time. | A table and graph of "fertile hybrid kits (%)" against "generations apart". |
| A species is a group that can breed and leave fertile offspring; real cases are not always clear-cut. | Reopen the crossing and read the hybrid outcome: born or not, fertile or not. | The reunion readout and a written judgement with a reason. |
| Chance, not only selection, changes allele frequencies (genetic drift, founder effect, bottleneck). | Storm sends 4 rabbits to an islet with the **same** climate as the mainland. Repeat with new seeds. | A dot plot of islet allele frequency across 20 runs next to the mainland value. |
| Gene flow moves alleles between populations and keeps them alike. | Carry rabbits across the flood every few generations and watch the hidden-difference meter. | Meter trace with and without carrying. |
| Working scientifically: predict, control variables, repeat trials, use data to support a conclusion, judge a model's limits. | Every investigation is predict, observe, explain. Seeds make repeat trials fair. The lab notes name the model's shortcuts. | Typed predictions and explanations, CSV exports. |

### 2.2 Misconceptions, and what the design does about each

| Misconception | Design answer |
|---|---|
| "Speciation happens in one moment (the flood made two species)." | The flood changes nothing about the rabbits. The hidden-difference meter starts at 0 and climbs slowly. The event banner says "Two populations, still one species". |
| "Rabbits change because they need to, or choose to." | No new mutation button. The lab starts with variation already present and the visible-gene mutation rate at 0, so every change is selection or chance on alleles that were already there. |
| "Populations that look different are different species." | Investigation B: eight generations apart gives a white alpine bank and a brown desert bank whose kits are perfectly healthy. |
| "Populations that look the same are the same species." | Investigation C: both banks keep the same climate and look alike, yet after about 40 generations most hybrid kits fail. |
| "Different species cannot mate at all." | Hybrids are conceived and often born. The readout separates three things: conceived, born alive, fertile. The mule comparison is in the text. Sterility arrives before inviability. |
| "There is a sharp line between one species and two." | The outcome has three bands, and the middle band is labelled "In between: biologists argue about cases like this". The clock is random, so two seeds held apart for the same time can land in different bands. |
| "Drift only happens in small populations." | The mainland line wobbles too, and the lab says so. The 20-run plot is repeated with 16 founders: the spread shrinks but does not vanish. Wording everywhere: drift happens in every population; it is **stronger** in small ones. |
| "The founders were the fittest / were chosen." | The four castaways are picked by the seeded random stream and their genotypes are shown beside the mainland frequencies at that instant. The islet has the same climate as the mainland. |
| "Isolation causes the change." | Text and report always name the cause: isolation only stops gene flow; selection and chance do the changing. |
| "Evolution has a goal; a new species is progress." | The challenge never rewards "making a species". Stars are for keeping both populations alive and for a correct, evidence-based prediction. |

### 4.2 Regions (land) and geometry

Three fixed regions. They exist for the whole islands run, whatever the water is doing.

| Index | Name | Where | Warrens (from `BURROWS`, unchanged) | Cap share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **West** | `x < streamX(z)` | (-13, 7), (-29, -12), (-23, 27), (-38, 4) | 270 |
| 1 | **East** | east of the stream and not on the islet | (19, 29), (8, -26), (30, -22) | 250 |
| 2 | **Islet** | `-10 < z < 22` and `streamX(z) < x < channelX(z)` | (24, 5) | 80 |

Side channel (an old creek bed that leaves the stream and rejoins it):

`channelX(z) = streamX(z) + 30 * sin(pi * (z + 10) / 32)` for `z` in `[-10, 22]`.

Checked numbers: the islet warren (24, 5) is 16.6 m from the stream centre and 8.0 m from the channel centre; the
channel's far shore never passes radius 41.5 (inside `ROAM`); the nearest East warren is 12.1 m from the channel; the
islet has about 280 m2 of dry land even with the river in flood. `BURROWS` is **not** edited (editing it would change
Survive's movement draws). The middle ford (`z = 4.2`) lands on the islet's west shore, so it is the West-Islet crossing.

`regionOf(x, z)` returns 0, 1 or 2 from the rules above. Each burrow gets a `region` in islands runs (derived, not
stored in `BURROWS`).

### 4.3 Water and links

Three switches, each 0 or 1, plus two eased levels for the picture.

| Switch | 0 | 1 | Link it controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| `river` | normal stream, half-width 3.5 m | flood, half-width `FLOOD_W = 7` m, all three fords under water | West-East (north and south fords) and West-Islet (middle ford) |
| `midFord` | stones in place | stones washed out | West-Islet only |
| `channel` | dry gully, walkable anywhere | full, half-width `CHANNEL_W = 3` m | Islet-East |

Link open rules: `WE = river == 0`; `WI = river == 0 && midFord == 0`; `IE = channel == 0`.

A **population** is a set of regions joined by open links (connected components, at most three). Order is by lowest
region index. Display name: region names joined with "+", except all three joined = "Valley", West+East = "Mainland".

| Scenario (lab preset and scripted use) | Switches | Populations |
|---|---|---|
| Open valley | 0, 0, 0 | Valley |
| **River flood** | river 1 | West / East+Islet |
| **Storm island** | channel 1, midFord 1 | Mainland / Islet |
| Three lands | river 1, channel 1 | West / East / Islet |

Reopening is the same switches going back to 0. The banner names it by climate: if both lands beside the crossing
have `snow >= 0.6` and `temp <= -0.4` it reads "The water has frozen over: an ice bridge"; otherwise "Drought: the
water is low and the crossing is back". For the channel: "A sandbar has formed: a land bridge to the islet". This is
wording and art only; the sim rule is identical.

**Timing (keeps runs frame-rate independent):** a water command is queued and takes effect at the **next generation
boundary**, like translocation does today. From the command until the boundary the cosmetic level eases toward its
target (so students see the water rising), and an `islwarn` event fires at once. At the boundary the switches flip,
populations are recomputed and a `split` or `rejoin` event fires.

`riverLevel` and `channelLevel` (0..1) are eased by sim time (`WATER_RAMP = 8` s for a full change). The keep-out
distance used by `nudge`, `spawn` and wander targets is `STREAM_W + (FLOOD_W - STREAM_W) * riverLevel + BANK` for the
river and `(CHANNEL_W + BANK) * channelLevel` for the channel (0 = no keep-out at all), so a rabbit standing where the
water spreads is pushed to the nearer shore at about half a metre per second, never teleported.

Sea-level rise and a landslide were considered and dropped for this update: the terrain is a saucer, so rising sea
would make one central lake and a ring of land, not islands; a landslide needs a second kind of barrier geometry.
Flood, storm channel, drought, ice bridge and sandbar cover the same science with one mechanism.

### 4.4 Membership and movement

- Every rabbit has `region` (0, 1, 2). It is set at spawn and re-read from position for every living rabbit at the
  start of each generation, before the history record and the hazards. Within a generation it does not change.
- A kit's region is parent A's region; its jittered position is clamped back into that region if needed.
- **Goals:** a rabbit only picks a home warren in a region its population can reach. Route rules for `setGoal`:

| From, to | Open valley route |
|---|---|
| West to East, or East to West | nearest of the north and south fords (existing logic, middle ford excluded) |
| West to Islet, or back | middle ford |
| Islet to East, or back | straight line (dry gully) |
| any pair whose link is closed | not allowed: the goal is replaced by the rabbit's own warren |

- **Wander:** the existing "four candidate spots near the home warren" rule stays, with three changes in islands
  runs: the test is "dry and in the warren's region"; the search radius is 20 m for West and East and 9 m for the
  Islet; six candidates instead of four, and if none is valid the target is the warren itself (today's code can
  pick an invalid spot when all fail, which on a small islet would send rabbits at the water).
- A rabbit caught on a ford when the flood commits uses the existing "stranded mid-ford: hop straight out" rule and
  joins whichever shore it reaches. The rule applies to the channel too.
- Foxes carry a `region`. Each region's fox count follows that region's `env.predators`; foxes enter from the rim
  point nearest their region, wander only inside it, and hunt only its rabbits. Valley-wide maximum stays 12.

### 4.5 Per-region environment

`sim.isl.regions[i].env = {snow, temp, food, tough, predators}` is the truth. `sim.setRegionEnv(i, partial)` and
`sim.setPopulationEnv(popIndex, partial)` (writes every region in that population) are the only setters.
`sim.env` stays on the object as a read-only **summary** (weights 0.5 / 0.4 / 0.1 for West / East / Islet) so old
readers such as the 2D view keep working; `setEnv` on an islands sim writes all three regions.

Hazards: `hazardContext(region)` builds the same context as today from that region's living rabbits, env, foxes,
shelters and clover. `scheduleHazards` loops regions 0, 1, 2 and, inside each, rabbits in `sim.rabbits` order, with
the same eight `rng` draws per rabbit as today. Fox kill spacing is per region. The fence halves foxes in one region
(the one chosen when it is bought).

Food and breeding are per **population**: food per head = (sum of `env.food` over its regions + reachable clover) /
(living rabbits in the population).

Presets for the lab (per population): **Meadow** (snow 0, temp 0.1, tough 0, foxes 0), **Alpine** (snow 0.9,
temp -0.7, tough 0, foxes 2), **Desert** (snow 0, temp 0.8, tough 0.7, foxes 1). Food is a separate "land size"
slider so a preset never changes how many rabbits a land supports.

### 4.6 Breeding while split, and the 600 cap

- When there is one population the existing `breed` runs untouched.
- Otherwise, for each population in order: take its fertile adults (`age >= 1`, not `sterile`), shuffle with `rng`,
  pair, and make kits with the existing kit-count rule using that population's food per head.
- **Cap:** each population's ceiling is the sum of `CAP_SHARE` over its regions (270 / 250 / 80, total 600). A
  population stops breeding at its own ceiling. Because the shares add to `P.CAP`, the valley can never pass 600 and
  no population can crowd out another. Room held for queued arrivals is charged to the population they are headed for.
- `P.CAP`, the renderer's 960 instance limit and `addAdults` / `queueAdults` are unchanged.

### 4.7 Hidden differences (the compatibility genome)

Each rabbit in an islands run carries a hidden diploid genome of `K = 30` two-state loci, stored as two 30-bit
integers `c0` and `c1`. They have **no effect** on survival, fur, ears or teeth. Founders start at `0, 0`.

**Inheritance.** A gamete from a parent takes, at each locus, one of that parent's two copies at random: one 30-bit
random mask per gamete (`gamete = (c0 & mask) | (c1 & ~mask)`). The kit gets one gamete from each parent.

**The clock (compressed time).** At the start of each generation, if the valley has more than one population, then
for each population in order:

1. Draw once. With probability `SUB_RATE = 0.16` this population gains one hidden change this generation.
2. If it does: list the loci that are the same in every living rabbit in the **whole valley** (both copies). Pick
   one at random. Set both copies of that locus, in every living member of **this** population, to the other value.
   If the list is empty, nothing happens.

So each change adds exactly one fixed difference between this population and every other one, and two populations
can never "cancel" each other while apart. A population with no living rabbits still makes its draw (keeps the
stream aligned) but changes nothing. The clock does not run in an open valley.

This is a deliberate shortcut and the lab says so in plain words: "In real life these hidden changes spread through
a population by chance over thousands of generations. Here that is squeezed into a few." The rate does not depend on
population size, which matches the standard result that neutral changes are fixed at the mutation rate whatever the
population size. (Drift on the three **visible** genes is real and individual-based, as it already is.)

**Distance between two populations** `D(p, q)` = sum over the 30 loci of `|f_p - f_q|`, where `f` is the frequency of
the 1 state in that population. For two never-mixed populations it equals the count of fixed differences. Expected
value while apart: `D ~ 2 * SUB_RATE * t = 0.32 t` (about 3 after 10 generations, 10 after 30, 19 after 60), with
real seed-to-seed spread. Capped at 30.

**Gene flow heals (compressed to match the clock).** When rabbits are **carried** from population A to population
B (section 4.9), then besides the rabbits themselves moving, `round(n * CARRY_HEAL)` loci (`CARRY_HEAL = 0.5`, so 2
for a group of four) at which A and B are fixed for different values are picked at random and B's living members
are set to A's value. Reason: the clock runs roughly thirty times faster than real drift, so a handful of migrants
would otherwise have no visible effect and the model would wrongly teach that gene flow does not matter. With this
rule, carrying a group every three generations holds `D` near zero, and never carrying lets it climb.

### 4.8 Hybrid viability and fertility

For every kit conceived in an islands run, mismatch `h = popcount(c0 XOR c1)` of the **kit** (the number of loci
where its two copies differ). Inside a never-mixed population `h = 0`. A first cross between populations at distance
`D` has `h = D`; a backcross about `D / 2`.

- **Viability** (is the kit born alive): `V(h) = 1 / (1 + (h / 12)^4)`.
- **Fertility** (can it breed as an adult): `F(h) = 1 / (1 + (h / 7)^4)`.

| h | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V born alive | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.94 | 0.84 | 0.67 | 0.50 | 0.24 | 0.11 |
| F fertile | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.90 | 0.65 | 0.37 | 0.19 | 0.10 | 0.04 | 0.01 |
| V x F | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.89 | 0.61 | 0.31 | 0.13 | 0.05 | 0.01 | 0.00 |

Fertility falls first, as in mules. Two draws per conceived kit: if the first is `>= V(h)` the kit is not born (no
spawn, no cap room used, a `stillborn` event and a counter); otherwise it is born and `kit.sterile = draw2 >= F(h)`.
Sterile rabbits live, eat and face hazards like any other, but are left out of pairing.

**Lineage tag.** `r.org` is a 3-bit mask. At every `split`, each living rabbit's `org` is reset to the bit of its
population. A kit's `org` is the OR of its parents'. A kit is a **cross kit** if its parents' `org` share no bit.
The tag is bookkeeping for the readout and the lineage view only; the biology comes from `c0`, `c1`.

**Species call** (used by the readout, the lab notes and the challenge). `Phi` = fertile live cross kits / cross kits
conceived, counted over a stated window (or from a test cross):

| Phi | Label shown |
|---|---|
| `>= 0.80` | "Still one species: they interbreed freely." |
| `0.20` to `0.80` | "In between: some hybrid kits fail or cannot breed. Biologists argue about cases like this." |
| `< 0.20` | "Two species (by the biological species concept): they can mate, but hybrids rarely survive and breed." |

With the default numbers the bands fall at roughly under 15, 15 to 28, and over 28 generations apart, but any one
seed can be earlier or later.

### 4.9 Player and teacher actions (sim API)

All take effect at the next generation boundary unless stated. All return `{ok, reason}` like `Challenge` actions.

| Call | Effect |
|---|---|
| `sim.setWater({river, midFord, channel})` | Queue new switch values. Emits `islwarn` now, `split` or `rejoin` at the boundary. |
| `sim.storm(n = 4, to = 2)` | Queue a storm: at the boundary pick `n` living adults at random (`rng` shuffle) from the population that holds West, and move them to the Islet. They keep their genotype, age and hidden genome; `home` becomes the islet warren. For three seconds they have a cosmetic `carry = {fromX, fromZ, toX, toZ}` so the renderer can show them swept across; the sim treats them as already on the islet. Refused if the islet is reachable on foot ("the islet is not cut off") or fewer than `n + 2` adults are available. Emits `storm`. |
| `sim.carry(fromPop, toPop, n = 4)` | Queue: move `n` random adults from one population to another (they walk out from the shore point nearest the destination warren), then apply the heal rule of 4.7. Capped by the destination's ceiling. Emits `carry`. |
| `sim.testCross(popA, popB, pairs = 12, kits = 3)` | **Immediate, read-only.** Samples fertile adults from each population (with replacement if short), builds the kits' hidden genomes, applies V and F, and returns `{gen, D, pairs, conceived, born, fertile, phi, meanH}`. No rabbit is created or changed. Appended to `sim.isl.crosses`. Emits `testcross`. Refused if either population has no fertile adult. |

### 4.10 Randomness and determinism

| Stream | Seed | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `rng` (existing) | `seed` | Unchanged uses, plus per-population adult shuffles and the storm / carry picks. |
| `mrng` (existing) | `seed ^ 0xA5A5A5A5` | Unchanged (movement). Cosmetic carry paths. |
| `crng` (new) | `seed ^ 0x3C6EF372` | The clock, hidden-genome gamete masks, the V and F draws, heal picks. |
| `xrng` (new) | `seed ^ 0x7F4A7C15` | `testCross` only, so **measuring never changes the run**. |

`crng` and `xrng` are only created when `this.isl` exists. Because every islands command commits at a generation
boundary and every draw happens in boundary code or in the fixed-order hazard loop, a run is fully determined by the
seed plus the list of `(generation, command)`; it does not depend on frame rate or substep size.

### 4.12 Sim-to-render contract additions

- `sim.isl` (null in classic modes): `{regions:[{id, name, env, cap}], water:{river, midFord, channel}, riverLevel,
  channelLevel, pops:[{regions:[...], name, n}], dist:{'0-1': n, ...} (current D per pair of populations),
  crosses:[...], splitGen, apartGens}`.
- Rabbit adds `region`, `org`, `sterile`, `c0`, `c1`, `carry` (or null). Predator adds `region`.
- New events on `sim.events`:

| `type` | Fields | When |
|---|---|---|
| `islwarn` | `{what: 'river'/'channel'/'midFord', to: 0/1, atGen}` | a water command is queued |
| `split` | `{pops:[{name, regions, n}], gen, t}` | the number of populations went up |
| `rejoin` | `{pops, joined:[nameA, nameB], D, kind: 'drought'/'ice'/'sandbar', gen, t}` | it went down |
| `storm` | `{ids, fromPop, to, founders:{b, e, T}, source:{b, e, T}, gen}` (allele frequencies of the four and of the source at that instant) | storm commits |
| `carry` | `{ids, from, to, healed, gen}` | carry commits |
| `stillborn` | `{parents:[id, id], x, z, h}` | a conceived kit was not viable |
| `hybrid` | `{id, sterile, h}` | a cross kit was born |
| `testcross` | the return value of `testCross` | on call |
| `popextinct` | `{pop, name, gen, t}` | a population reaches 0 while others live |

### 4.13 History and data

## 5. Challenge: Two Lineages 10:00

**Brief shown to the student:** "A flood is coming. It will split the warren in two. Keep **both** lineages alive
until the water drops. Then find out: are they still one species?"

### 5.2 Ranger tools (same trust economy as Survive: start 12, +1.5 per generation, +0.5 diversity bonus)

| Tool | Cost | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Clover patch | 4 | as Survive; feeds only rabbits inside the ring, so it serves the bank it is placed on |
| Brush shelter | 5 | as Survive |
| Fox fence | 8 | as Survive, for **one bank** (two buttons); cooldown 10 generations per bank |
| **Carry across** | 6 | carry 4 adults West to East or East to West (two buttons); cooldown 3 generations; only while the flood is up. This is the only gene flow: no outside source populations in this challenge. |

Carry across is the central trade-off. It can rescue a bank that has lost an allele it needs, and each group also
pulls the lineages back together (the heal rule). Late in the split, carried rabbits' kits are themselves often
sterile, so a late rescue works worse than an early one. Nothing in the UI calls either choice right.

### 5.3 Ending, prediction and stars

- **Lose:** either lineage reaches 0 before 8:00. Headline: "The East lineage died out at 5:12, generation 31. With
  one lineage left there is nothing to compare." The report still opens.
- **Prediction stop (7:30):** the sim pauses; a card shows the two populations' allele bars, the hidden-difference
  meter and any test crosses the student ran (one free test cross is offered here if they ran none). The student
  picks one of the three species labels and types one sentence of reason. It cannot be skipped; it can be reopened
  and changed until 8:00.
- **Outcome:** `Phi` over all cross kits conceived from 8:00 to 10:00. If fewer than 10 were conceived, a 12-pair
  test cross taken at 8:00 (on `xrng`) is used and the report says so.
- **Stars:** (1) both lineages have at least 6 rabbits at 8:00; (2) both have at least 30; (3) the prediction
  matches the outcome band. Star 3 needs star 1.

### 5.4 Report

Same overlay and reflection storage as Survive's end screen. Contents: headline; stars; **side-by-side
allele-frequency charts** for West and East with event bands and a shaded "apart" span; hidden-difference line
with test-cross dots; reunion table (cross kits conceived / born alive / fertile, and `Phi`); the student's
prediction and reason next to the outcome; three generated sentences; three reflection questions.

Sentence rules (same evidence discipline as `buildReport`): state the observed change first ("While apart, white fur
rose from 28 % to 81 % in the West and fell from 31 % to 6 % in the East"), then death-record evidence per bank,
then other causes (carried rabbits, small numbers). Never write that the flood, isolation or need caused an allele
to change; never write "became a new species at"; always give the hybrid numbers before the label.

Reflection questions:
1. "The two banks started with the same alleles. Why did they end up different? Use one piece of evidence from each graph."
2. "At the moment of the flood, were there two species? What changed between then and the reunion, and what did not?"
3. "Your result was '<label>'. What would you need to observe to be more sure? Why do biologists sometimes disagree about whether two populations are one species?"

### 5.5 As built: balance tuning (2026-11-14)

The first build had no real decision in it: every strategy kept both lineages alive 40 to 65 % of the time. Starvation
caused most deaths (each bank overshot its food, crashed, and hitting 0 was boom-bust luck), and the tools barely
changed the outcome. What changed, all in `src/sim/lineages.js` (`L`, the `LIN` block) and in `IslandSim` behind
`islands.lin` (null for the lab and every classic mode):

- **Litter size follows food per head** (`_linKits`): `kit0 + kitSlope * (food / n - 1)`, clamped to 0 to 4. A bank sits
  near what its land feeds and a crash refills quickly, so starvation stops deciding the run.
- **Hard spells are lethal to the mismatched phenotype** (`coldX`, `heatX`, fox counts): long ears in the West freeze,
  short ears in the East overheat, brown rabbits on snow are caught. Starting allele frequencies are lower (b 0.18, e 0.13,
  T 0.20, 26 founders) so a bank can really lack an allele.
- **Tools are strong and have a budget**: fence takes foxes to 0.48 for 9 generations (8 trust, cooldown 10 per bank),
  brush (3 trust, radius 20, 6 generations) cuts fox, cold and heat deaths by 70 %, clover (3 trust, radius 16, 5 generations)
  feeds up to 45 rabbits in its ring. Income is 1.5 trust a generation, so a student cannot cover both banks all the time:
  the forecast says which bank to cover next.
- **Benchmark `sidePlanner`** now uses only the HUD: forecast strip (`current`/`next`), per-bank counts and allele bars,
  trust, button states and the rings on screen. It no longer reads fox counts.

Results (share of runs with both lineages at least 6 at 8:00):

| Player | 40 seeds `AUD000-039` | 40 fresh seeds `QQQ000-039` |
|---|---|---|
| none | 15 % | 20 % |
| sidePlanner | 88 % | 98 % |
| carrySpam / cloverSpam / shelterSpam / fenceSpam | 13 / 38 / 28 / 23 % | 15 / 35 / 30 / 48 % |
| panicky | 33 % | 35 % |

`none` never earns 3 stars. B5: `sidePlanner` labels one/between/two = 6/14/15 (AUD) and 8/18/13 (QQQ). B6: median `D`
at 8:00 is 0 to 1 with carrying and 9 to 10 without. B7 (late rescue) is not tested.

## 6. Renderer

### 6.1 Water

- **Stream ribbon** (`buildStream`): build it once at flood width (half-width 10 m) with a per-vertex signed
  distance from the centre line. New uniforms `uHalfW` (visible half-width, 4.05 to 7.6 m) and `uLift` (0 to
  0.75 m). The fragment shader computes the foam edge from distance / `uHalfW` and discards beyond it. At
  `riverLevel = 0` the picture must match today's stream.
- **Flood look:** the water widens and rises over the eased level, turns a silty green-brown (`uSilt`), flows about
  twice as fast, with a few drifting foam streaks. The stepping stones (instanced in `props.js`) sink 0.5 m and
  fade; when the river drops they rise wet and dark, then dry.
- **Side channel:** a second ribbon with the same material and its own uniforms, following `channelX(z)`. Dry: not
  drawn. Filling: a water front runs from the upstream mouth to the downstream mouth over the ramp (`uFill`).
- **Terrain (`heightAt`, all modes):** add (a) a shallow floodplain shelf, 0.25 m deep, from 3.5 to 9 m either side
  of the stream, so flood water has a bed and a clean shoreline; (b) the channel as a dry gully 0.45 m deep with a
  pebbly bed (reuse the "wet bank" ground paint at half strength). Both are picture-only: the sim never reads
  height. Trees, rocks and burrow mounds must not be scattered inside the shelf or gully (extend the existing
  stream exclusion in `props.js`). In classic modes the gully reads as an old creek bed and rabbits walk through it.
- **Reopening:** drought = water narrows, stones return; ice bridge = the ribbon between the banks whitens with a
  frosted, slightly raised surface and the stones stay hidden (rabbits cross on the ford lines as usual); sandbar =
  a pale wedge across the middle of the channel, drawn as two short terrain-coloured decal strips while the channel
  elsewhere drains.

### 6.2 Per-region ground

`paint.js` gains `uIsl` (0 or 1), `uRegSnow`, `uRegTemp`, `uRegDry` (vec3: West, East, Islet) and one GLSL helper
`envAt(xz)` that returns `(snow, temp, dry)` for a world position: region weights from the same `streamX` and
`channelX` formulas, blended over 3 m centred on each water line (the seam is under water or on the bank). Every
material that reads `uSnow / uTemp / uDry` (ground, grass, trees, rocks) calls `envAt` instead; with `uIsl = 0` it
returns the globals, so classic modes render exactly as now. The formulas are emitted into GLSL from the constants
in `valley.js` so sim and picture cannot drift apart.

Snowfall particles are thinned per particle by the snow of the region they are over. Sky, fog and light tint follow
the region under the camera target, eased over 1.5 s. Heat shimmer follows the same value.

### 6.3 Rabbits, foxes, cues

- No change to instancing or LOD. `carry`: the rabbit is drawn along an arc low over the water with a spin and a
  splash puff at each end (3 s).
- **Lineage view** (new toggle, exclusive with genotype view): ground rings by `org`: West teal, East amber, Islet
  violet, mixed ancestry white; sterile rabbits get a grey ring. Uses the existing ring instances.
- `stillborn`: a small pale puff at the parents and nothing else. `hybrid`: a brief ring flash on the kit.
- `split`: `world.pulse` pull-back, a slow glance along the river, both banks pinged. `rejoin`: glance at the nearest ford.

### 6.4 Camera

`world.frame(target)` where target is `null` (whole valley, the current home view), a region index, or a population
(list of regions): eases the orbit target and distance to fit that land. Islet framing: target (22, 6), distance 26.
The free-view clamp is unchanged.

### 6.5 Performance budget

At most two extra draw calls (channel ribbon, sandbar decals). No new render targets. No per-frame allocation in the
islands paths. 600 rabbits with the river in flood and the channel full must hold the same tier as 600 rabbits do in
Survive today on the same machine. Low tier drops the silt streaks and the per-particle snow thinning.

### 6.6 2D fallback (`src/ui/debugView.js`)

Draw the river at its current width, the channel when filling or full, a faint tint per region for snow / dry,
lineage-view dot outlines, and the three crossings as open / closed marks. The lab and the challenge must be fully
usable with `?view=2d`.

| U17 | Founder effect | `founderTrial`, 60 seeds: standard deviation of islet `freq_b` at +15 generations is at least 3 times the mainland's across the same runs; with 16 founders the islet's is smaller than with 4 but still above the mainland's; mean islet `freq_b` is within 0.08 of the mainland mean (no hidden bias: it is chance, not selection). |
| U18 | Drift is everywhere | Across those 60 runs the mainland `freq_b` standard deviation is greater than 0. |
| U19 | Determinism | Same seed + same `(generation, command)` list gives an identical fingerprint with step sizes 0.25, 0.1 and 1/60; a different seed differs. |
| U20 | History and CSV | Region blocks sum to the valley totals every generation (rabbits, births, each death cause); the populations CSV has `3 x generations` data rows and parses back to the same numbers; classic `toCSV` output for a classic run is unchanged. |
| U21 | Events | One `split` per increase in population count and one `rejoin` per decrease, in order, each at a generation boundary; `islwarn` precedes each. |

## 13. Risks and decisions (all decided)

| # | Question or risk | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lab inside Explore, or its own mode? | Own title card "Islands" with a chooser: lab + challenge. | Explore's two-rabbit start and single climate do not fit; one card keeps the title screen at four. |
| 2 | Which separation events? | Water only: river flood and a storm-filled side channel. Reopen by drought, ice bridge or sandbar (wording and art). | One barrier mechanism. Sea-level rise does not make islands in a saucer-shaped valley; landslide needs new geometry. |
| 3 | How many populations? | Up to three, on three fixed regions. Feature 1 and the challenge use two; the islet is the third. | "Two or more" is met without free-form geometry. |
| 4 | Hidden divergence as real neutral drift on many loci, or a clock? | A per-population clock that fixes one of 30 hidden loci at a time, carried by individuals and inherited normally. | True neutral drift needs far more generations than a lesson has (divergence needs time much longer than population size). The clock gives a result in 20 to 50 generations, and because the loci sit in individuals, hybrids, backcrosses and mixing after reunion all behave sensibly without extra rules. The shortcut is stated on screen. |
| 5 | Should the clock run faster in small populations? | No. Same rate for every population. | Matches the neutral-rate result, and keeps "time apart" as the one variable in investigation 3. Small-population effects are taught with the visible genes, where drift is real. |
| 6 | Gene flow would barely slow a compressed clock. | Carried groups also erase 0.5 differences per rabbit. | Without it the model would teach that gene flow does not matter. Listed in "What this model leaves out". |
| 7 | One number for hybrids, or two? | Two: viability and fertility, fertility failing first. | It is what breaks "different species cannot mate" and gives the mule case. |
| 8 | Sharp species threshold? | Three bands with a named grey zone, and a random clock. | The grey zone is the lesson. |
| 9 | Show the meter's meaning directly? | No species words on the meter; the label appears only on cross results. | Keeps the test cross a real experiment and the challenge prediction a real judgement. |
| 10 | The 600 cap with several populations | Fixed per-region shares (270 / 250 / 80). | Simple, deterministic, fair; never above 600. |
| 11 | Old modes changing by accident | Everything behind `sim.isl`; new random streams; golden test first. | Survive's balance took real effort; it must not move. |
| 12 | Terrain edits (shelf and gully) alter the look of old modes | Accept, picture only. | Rebuilding terrain per mode costs load time and code; the gully foreshadows the feature. Checked by R1. |
| 13 | Per-region shader seams | 3 m blend placed on the water lines; formulas generated from `valley.js`. | The seam is always under water or bank. |
| 14 | Rabbits stuck or jittering at a small islet's shores | Region-aware wander with a smaller radius and a "go home" fallback; U3 checks keep-out time. | Today's fallback can target invalid ground. |
| 15 | Islet population dies out, ruining a founder lesson | Allow it, say so (banner), and offer "Next seed". Islet has no foxes by default. | Extinction of small populations is true and worth seeing; the 20-run plot counts it. |
| 16 | Reunion crashes both populations (half of all pairs waste their kits) | Allow it. The challenge's stars are decided at 8:00, before the reunion. | It is the real cost of hybridising; it is not punished. |
| 17 | Outside translocation in the challenge | Removed for Two Lineages; Carry across is the only gene flow. | Outside rabbits would need an invented hidden genome and would blur the two-lineage story. |
| 18 | Students rush the 15-generation waits | Speed up to 4x, "Jump 10 generations", and "Run 20 quick islands". | A lesson is 50 minutes. |
| 19 | Mate choice and reinforcement | Not modelled; named in "What this model leaves out". | Out of scope; see Future. |
| 20 | Syllabus wording | Paraphrased here; owner checks against the school program before student-facing use. | Avoids quoting a dot point wrongly. |
| 21 | Third population's graphs on a phone | Panels stack vertically under 760 px; the drawer scrolls. | Three side-by-side panels do not fit 375 px. |
| 22 | Challenge length | 10:00 (60 generations): 1:30 together, 6:30 apart, 2:00 reunion. | 39 generations apart puts most uncarried runs near or past the two-species band while leaving real spread (B5). |
